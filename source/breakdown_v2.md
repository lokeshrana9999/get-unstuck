# Getting Started with Executive Dysfunction: breakdown of the flowchart (v2)

The chart has a **page 1** (left part) and a **page 2** (right part). Boxes link across with "see pg 2" and "cont'd from page 1". For this version, every connector was checked on zoomed crops of the image. Arrowheads mark where a line ends and small flat ticks mark where it starts, so every routing below is traced directly. None are left unclear.

Quoted text follows the chart's own spelling and punctuation, including its hyphens (e.g. "Scientist Hat"- …) and "Accomodate" [sic].

---

## 1. Overall logic (summary, not chart text)

- **① Do you know what you need to do?** If you're overwhelmed, PRIORITIZE. If you know what to do after that, you're told to move to Step 2. Only if you're still overwhelmed do you go on to BREAK IT DOWN.
- **② Can you start the task?** If yes: *Hell yeah, get started!* If not, go to ③.
- **③ Why are you having trouble getting started?** Each reason has its own first-line strategy:
  - inertia → INTERRUPT INERTIA (pg 2)
  - impulse control → RESIST IMPULSES (pg 2)
  - motivation → "Can you do it for just 10 minutes?"
  - not sure why → MINDFULNESS
  - sensory/emotional regulation → WELLBEING
  - perfectionism or paralysis → BREAK IT DOWN

  Interrupt Inertia and Resist Impulses are page-2 boxes, but they're first-line strategies reached directly from ③, not fallbacks.
- **When a strategy doesn't work**, the chart sends you somewhere else. Usually that's **④ Are there other reasons you can't start the task?**, but not always:
  - Make It More Tolerable → DIY DOPAMINE
  - Mindfulness ("I still don't know…") → WELLBEING
  - Break It Down ("still feels too hard") → WELLBEING
  - Resist Impulses ("sort of resisting… but not starting") → INTERRUPT INERTIA
- **④** asks whether there are other reasons and whether you actually tried the suggestions. Only "I did, I promise!" leads on to ⑤.
- **⑤** asks whether the task really has to be done now. Its five endpoints are summarized here with my own labels (not chart text): reschedule, delegate, do it partly, push through, accept.
- **Successes end in one of two ways:**
  - a *"Hell yeah, get started!"* starburst
  - a "Great job / Go to Step N" redirect: "Great job! Move to step 2.", "Great job! Go to Step 3.", and the two Wellbeing "…Go to Step 2." starbursts

## 2. Decision nodes and page-1 strategies

### ① Do you know what you need to do?
- **Yes!** → ②
- **OVERWHELM: "Kind of, but I'm not sure where to start"** → PRIORITIZE
- **OVERWHELM: "No idea :("** → PRIORITIZE

### ② Can you start the task?
- **Yes!** → *Hell yeah, get started!*
- **"No, that's why I'm using this"** → ③

### ③ Why are you having trouble getting started?
| Branch | Leads to |
|---|---|
| **INERTIA:** "I want to do it but I can't stop doing something else (see pg 2)" | "INERTIA: … (cont'd from page 1)" → INTERRUPT INERTIA (pg 2) |
| **IMPULSE CONTROL:** "I **really** want to do something else specifically (see pg 2)" | "IMPULSE CONTROL: … (cont'd from page 1)" → RESIST IMPULSES (pg 2) |
| **MOTIVATION:** "I don't want to do it" | "Can you do it for just 10 minutes?" |
| **??? Not sure why** | MINDFULNESS |
| **SENSORY & EMOTIONAL REGULATION:** "I feel bad, upset, gross" | WELLBEING |
| **PERFECTIONISM:** "It feels too hard or I'm afraid of failing" | BREAK IT DOWN (dotted line) |
| **PARALYSIS:** "I don't know how to do it" | BREAK IT DOWN (dotted line) |

### Can you do it for just 10 minutes?
- **"Yeah, that sounds doable"** → *Hell yeah, get started!* This is the same page-1 starburst that "One of those worked!" from MAKE IT MORE TOLERABLE reaches via the long connector up the left edge.
- **"That still sounds bad"** → **Why?**
  - **"It's an unpleasant or arduous task"** → MAKE IT MORE TOLERABLE
  - **"I'm worried I'll fail."** → "Go to Step 4." (starburst)

### PRIORITIZE
- Use an important/urgent matrix
- Pick the top 3 things or 1 next thing

Outcomes:
- **"I know what to do now!"** → "Great job! Move to step 2."
- **"OVERWHELM: I still don't know…"** → BREAK IT DOWN

### BREAK IT DOWN
Reached from PRIORITIZE ("I still don't know…") and, by dotted lines, from PERFECTIONISM and PARALYSIS.
- Use Goblin Tools!
- What's the smallest, easiest, **measurable** first step?

Outcomes:
- **"I know what to do now!"** → "Great job! Move to step 2." This is the same starburst PRIORITIZE uses.
- **"I still don't know :("** → "Revisit previous to-do lists, ask for help, or take a walk break to think about it. Go to Step 1."
- A dotted line leads to a separate box, **"It still feels too hard or like too much"**, and from there a dotted line runs to WELLBEING.

### MINDFULNESS
"Take 5 minutes to sit quietly and consider why. How about now?"
- **"I know why now!"** → "Great job! Go to Step 3."
- **"I still don't know…"** → WELLBEING

### MAKE IT MORE TOLERABLE
- **Listen** to music or an audiobook
- **Accomodate the task** [sic] to make it less bad
- **Pretend to be excited** about it, **find the fun**
- **Decide on a reward** for after
- **Change of scenery**
- **Wear the "Scientist Hat"**- they do this task!
- **Body double** with a friend

Outcomes:
- **"One of those worked!"** → *Hell yeah, get started!* (the shared page-1 starburst, via the left-edge connector)
- **"This didn't help (see pg 2)"** → "Making it tolerable didn't help (cont'd from page 1)" → DIY DOPAMINE

### WELLBEING
Reached from three places:
- SENSORY & EMOTIONAL REGULATION
- MINDFULNESS → "I still don't know…"
- the dotted "It still feels too hard or like too much" box

"Are your physical & emotional needs met right now?"
- **"Needs are met! (see pg 2)"** → "Needs are met! (cont'd from page 1)" → ④
- **"No, I need something. It's…"** → one of:
  - **EMOTIONAL:** social interaction, managing shame, fear, anxiety, etc
  - **PHYSICAL:** meds, food, rest sensory, water, movement, etc
- Both lead to **"Can the need be met right now, even partially?"**
  - **Yes!**
    - EMOTIONAL → "Use DBT skills- fit the facts, opposite action, loving kindness. Go to Step 2."
    - PHYSICAL → "Attend to the need. Go to Step 2"
  - **"Need can't be met (see page 2)"** → "Need can't be met (cont'd from page 1)" → ④

## 3. Page-2 strategies

### INTERRUPT INERTIA
Reached from two places:
- "INERTIA: I want to do it but I can't stop doing something else (cont'd from page 1)"
- RESIST IMPULSES → "I'm sort of resisting the impulse, but not starting the task.", via the long connector up the left side of page 2

Bullets:
- **Create interruptions:** drinking lots of water, content blockers, etc
- **Stop what you're doing for just 5 minutes**
- **Drink water** so you get up to pee
- **Put on content blocker**
- **Get into an uncomfortable position** so you're more likely to get up
- **Set a recurring alarm** to check in: do you want to be doing this?
- **Ask for help** to interrupt task
- **Set up accountability** from others
- **Make grumbling noises** about it
- **Pretend you're teaching** someone ADHD skills & verbally explain how you overcome paralysis

Outcomes (one line leaves the bottom of the box and splits three ways):
- **"I got unstuck from the task, but still need help starting."** → DIY DOPAMINE
- **"I can start the task now!"** → *Hell yeah, get started!*
- **"These didn't help"** → "Create a harmless but immediate **problem** (toss phone across room, knock over water) Go to step 4."

### DIY DOPAMINE
Reached from two places:
- "Making it tolerable didn't help (cont'd from page 1)"
- "I got unstuck from the task, but still need help starting." (from INTERRUPT INERTIA)

Bullets:
- **Dance break** with fun song
- **Delicious snack**
- **Exercise:** 60s jumping jacks
- **Stretch**
- **Novelty** (position, environs)

Outcomes:
- **"I can start the task now!"** → *Hell yeah, get started!* This box is shared with INTERRUPT INERTIA.
- **"It isn't helping"** → ④

### RESIST IMPULSES
Reached from "IMPULSE CONTROL: I **really** want to do something else specifically (cont'd from page 1)".
- **Name the emotion, impulse, and need.** "I'm feeling grumpy, so I want to give up. I need to have a snack and take a break."
- **Create physical distance** from the thing
- **Create a gap.** Wait just 5 minutes before acting.
- **Look back:** During those 5 minutes, think about the last time you acted on this impulse. ("Last time I ___, I felt ___ / ____ happened")
- **Look forward:** "How will I feel if I follow X impulse?" Answer that question for yourself, as well as "How will I feel if I don't follow X impulse?" (let there be good feelings about that one too!)
- **Find an out:** add a natural stopping point- drink lots of water so you need to get up to pee, or set a content blocker to start in 15 minutes.
- **Plan an interruption** or ask a friend for help interrupting.
- **Make it harder** to follow impulse- delete apps, hide things, etc.
- **Set a recurring alarm** to check in: do you want to be doing this?
- **Set up accountability** with others
- **Follow the dopamine** and replace it with something shorter/more contained- rather than play the game, do the task but get a snack from the vending machine after.
- **Pretend you're teaching** someone ADHD skills & verbally explain how you resist impulses

Outcomes:
- **"One of those worked!"** → *Hell yeah, get started!*
- **"I'm sort of resisting the impulse, but not starting the task."** → INTERRUPT INERTIA, via the left-side connector
- **"I still can't resist, I really really want to do the thing"** → a comic back-and-forth, which runs strictly in this order:
  1. → "Go to Step 4." (starburst)
  2. → red box: "I don't want to, I really want to do this thing instead and I don't want to bother with skills."
  3. → "Do DBT worksheet on **willfulness.** Go to Step 4."
  4. → "I don't want to!!"
  5. → "Please go to Step 4?"
  6. → "No!!!"
  7. → "Fine."
  8. → "**Practice radical acceptance.** This task may not get done right now. It might feel like a catastrophe, but it'll be okay." This is the same starburst as ⑤'s "Not worth suffering for" endpoint.

## 4. Steps ④ and ⑤ (pg 2)

### ④ Are there other reasons you can't start the task?
Reached from:
- "Need can't be met (cont'd from page 1)"
- "Needs are met! (cont'd from page 1)"
- DIY DOPAMINE → "It isn't helping"
- the various "Go to Step 4" starbursts

Branches:
- **Yes** → "Go through the flowchart again to solve them first"
- **Nope** → "Did you *actually* follow/try the suggestions on the flowchart?"
  - **"I may have skipped some"** → "Go through the flowchart again to solve them first" (the same starburst as **Yes**)
  - **"I did, I promise!"** → ⑤

### ⑤ Do *you* need to *complete* the task *right now*? Can you get an extension or make more time?
- **It can wait** → "**Set another day/time** to complete the task"
- **No flexibility** → "Can the task be delegated? Can someone else help you?"
  - **Yes!** → "Ask for help!"
  - **Nope** → "Can you do the task partially or badly?"
    - **Yes!** → "**Half-ass it.** Something is better than nothing!"
    - **"No, it's high-stakes"** → "Is it really worth pushing yourself to do it anyway?"
      - **"Not worth suffering for"** → "**Practice radical acceptance.** This task may not get done right now. It might feel like a catastrophe, but it'll be okay."
      - **"It's worth it"** → "Do the task. Use **distress tolerance** skills and harm reduction tools to minimize its impact on you."

Summary labels for the five endpoints (my paraphrase, not chart text): **reschedule**, **delegate**, **do it partly**, **push through** (distress tolerance and harm reduction), **accept** (radical acceptance).

## 6. How a person might use it (interpretation, not chart text)

1. **Start at ①.** If you don't know what to do, prioritize: use an important/urgent matrix, and pick the top 3 things or 1 next thing. If you know what to do now, move to Step 2. If you're still overwhelmed, break it down to the smallest measurable first step.
2. **At ②**, if you can start, go.
3. **If you can't, name the reason at ③** and use the strategy for it:
   - **Unmotivated:** try just 10 minutes.
     - If the task is unpleasant, make it more tolerable, then try DIY dopamine.
     - If you're worried you'll fail, go to Step 4.
   - **Not sure why:** sit quietly for 5 minutes. If you still don't know, check your wellbeing.
   - **Feel bad:** check your physical and emotional needs and meet them, even partly.
   - **Perfectionism or paralysis:** break it down. If it still feels like too much, check your wellbeing.
   - **Stuck doing something else:** interrupt inertia. If you get unstuck but still can't start, try DIY dopamine.
   - **Craving something specific:** resist impulses. If you're resisting but still not starting, treat it as inertia.
4. **If you're still stuck, go to ④.** Look for other reasons, and be honest about whether you actually tried the suggestions.
5. **At ⑤, decide what to do about the task itself:** reschedule, delegate, do it badly, push through with distress tolerance, or accept it won't get done right now.

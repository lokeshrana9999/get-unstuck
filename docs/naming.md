# Naming: does the current name still fit?

*Written 2026-10-31 for the pivot in [PIVOT.md](PIVOT.md). Current name: **"Get Unstuck: Task Starter for ADHD & Executive Dysfunction"** (see [bot/persona.md](../bot/persona.md)). No trademark searches have been run; every option below needs one.*

## Assessment of the current name

The bot used to be a helper for the moment someone is stuck. After the pivot it also does onboarding and goal capture, scheduled start nudges, optional morning plans, focus (body-doubling-style) sessions and end-of-day reviews. The flowchart is now the in-the-moment engine inside a wider productivity companion.

**Pros**
- **Discoverable.** Listings often show only the name (Grok Bot documents no character limits, so truncation behaviour is unknown; see [research-grok-bot.md](research-grok-bot.md) §1). "ADHD" and "Executive Dysfunction" are the words people search for, and "Task Starter" says plainly what it does.
- **"Get Unstuck" is still true.** Starting is still the core problem the bot solves, and the stuck-flow is still its most distinctive feature (task breakdown is the feature people value most in the app landscape; [research-adhd-productivity.md](research-adhd-productivity.md) Part B §3, lesson 8).
- **Continuity.** Lokesh chose it for discoverability ([verdict-response.md](verdict-response.md), point 8), and the repo, README and skills already use it.
- **Makes no clinical claim.** "Task Starter" describes an action, not a treatment.

**Cons**
- **Undersells the new scope.** "Task Starter" says nothing about planning, reminders, focus sessions or reviews. Someone looking for an ADHD planner or reminder bot may skip it.
- **Framed around a problem state.** "Unstuck" implies you arrive already stuck; the bot now also contacts people proactively before they are stuck.
- **Long.** At about 60 characters it is the most likely to be truncated in a listing, and the truncated part would be the part that carries the search terms.
- **"Executive Dysfunction" sounds clinical** and sits close to diagnostic language, even though the persona says the bot doesn't assume ADHD. Putting two condition labels in the name leans harder on the audience than the honest pitch ("a productivity companion built on reasonable, general-population principles; mostly untested in ADHD adults") needs.

**Verdict:** it still works, especially for search, but it describes the old product better than the new one. A rename is optional, not urgent.

## Option 1: Get Unstuck: ADHD Planner & Gentle Nudges

- **Rationale:** keeps the established "Get Unstuck" brand, keeps "ADHD" for search, and names the two new things the bot does (planning and reminders). "Gentle" signals the no-pressure, no-escalation design. Shorter than the current name.
- **Risks:**
  - *Clinical-sounding:* low. "Planner" and "nudges" are everyday words. "ADHD" in the name still invites people to assume it is an ADHD product; the description must keep the "doesn't assume ADHD" and "not medical advice" lines.
  - *Trademark check needed:* "ADHD Planner" is a common descriptive phrase used by many apps and paper planners; check for conflicts in app stores and template galleries.
  - *Claims:* "Planner" implies calendar-style planning; the bot only *proposes* calendar writes, so the description must be clear about that.
- **Label:** Plans, nudges, and help when you're stuck

## Option 2: Get Unstuck: Start, Focus & Finish (ADHD-friendly)

- **Rationale:** describes the whole loop (start nudges, focus sessions, end-of-day review) in plain verbs. "ADHD-friendly" signals the audience without implying the user has ADHD or that the bot treats it.
- **Risks:**
  - *Clinical-sounding:* low.
  - *Trademark check needed:* "Start, Focus & Finish" is generic but may resemble existing productivity course or book titles.
  - *Claims:* "Finish" promises an outcome the evidence can't back (almost nothing here is tested in ADHD adults). "ADHD-friendly" is a soft claim and should be checked with ADHD users before use. The parenthesis may be cut off in listings, losing the search term.
- **Label:** Start, focus and finish, gently

## Option 3: Get Unstuck: Task Starter & Check-ins for ADHD Brains

- **Rationale:** the smallest change from the current name. Keeps "Task Starter", adds "Check-ins" for the proactive side, and swaps "Executive Dysfunction" for warmer community language.
- **Risks:**
  - *Clinical-sounding:* lower than now, but "ADHD Brains" is a neuro-framing the bot otherwise avoids, and may read as assuming the user has ADHD.
  - *Trademark check needed:* "ADHD Brain(s)" and similar phrases are used by coaching businesses and media; check before use.
  - *Claims:* "for ADHD Brains" hints at an ADHD-specific design or effect, which the evidence doesn't support. Loses the "Executive Dysfunction" search term for people without a diagnosis.
- **Label:** Gentle starts and check-ins

## Recommendation

**Option 1, "Get Unstuck: ADHD Planner & Gentle Nudges"**, if Lokesh wants the name to match the new scope. It keeps the brand and the main search term, covers the proactive features, is shorter (less likely to truncate) and drops the most clinical-sounding phrase. If discoverability for people without an ADHD diagnosis matters more, keep the current name until there is listing data. Whichever is chosen, run a trademark check and keep the "not medical advice" and "doesn't assume you have ADHD" lines in the description.

No rename has been made; this is a proposal for Lokesh to decide.
